===== halfstop
SUMMARY: I found one new condition for the tight (half) stop, and it fixes the 2 genuinely tight stops the model got wrong in the student's log without changing anything on the screenshot set. The rule: the first impulse of a session in a given direction also gets the tight stop when the session OPENED outside the previous session's range against that direction (long: session open < previous session low; short: session open > previous session high). This is an OR with the existing counter-bias test, which stays as it is (net impulse count of the previous session at least 3 the other way). It is option tightOut (default off) in /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Bank-Loan-Report/21ed2550-acec-5cf1-bf01-a24cbf22fe18/scratchpad/hunt2/halfstop/m.py.

Scores I computed (score2.py, plus my A-set stop checker astop.py):
- Defaults: A 79/1/3, B 32 matched with 29/32 stops right (same as the baseline).
- {"tightOut":true}: A 79/1/3, and all 79 matched A stops unchanged. B 32 matched with 31/32 stops right: #49 and #56 are fixed.
- {"tightOut":true,"dayStopR":0}: A 79/1/3, B 36 matched with 35/36 stops right.
- The tight stop formula itself, entry -/+ max(0.9 ATR, distance to the signal candle's low/high), reproduces the logged stops: #45 8748.1 vs 8748, #49 8742.0 vs 8741, #51 8844.5 vs 8844, #56 8991.5 vs 8992.

Checking the logged stops against the logged exit time and highest TP (exitchk.py):
- #45, #49, #51 and #56 are truly tight. One caveat: for #49 the Excel computes the TP levels from the student's own stop, so its "TP3" does not prove anything on its own.
- #58 and #60 are NORMAL 1.8 ATR stops; the logged stops are student errors. For #58, the logged 9098 (and the tight 9103.9) would have been hit at 19:05 after TP1, but the log says SL at 19:20 with no TP. The normal stop 9083.3 gives exactly 19:20 with no TP. For #60, the logged 9234 would exit at 12:40, while the normal 9236.2 gives the logged 12:05. So 1.14 ATR and 2.03 ATR are not real stop modes.
- #52 is neither formula. Its stop is not the signal candle's high (8827); the tight value 8829.2 would exit at 11:05, not the logged 11:10. The exit at 11:10 needs a stop between 8834 and 8841. The normal stop 8842.4 misses the 11:10 high (8841) by only 1.4 points, so it is most likely a normal stop with a small data difference plus a 3-point logging error.

Why I trust the new rule:
- Among the 30 first-same-direction-impulse cases in A and B, the session opened outside the previous range against the trade direction only for #49 and #56, and both are tight. That is 2 for, 0 against, and no normal trade meets the condition.
- No threshold to fit; it is the standard "opened below yesterday's low / above yesterday's high" test you would write in Pine.
- It fires rarely: CRUDE 2026-09-21 11:25 L, #49 and #56, plus a knock-on new trade at 10-14 12:15; 4 NIFTY trades in September; none in other symbols and none inside A windows. Because so few cases test it, the evidence is real but thin.

Rejected or weaker options:
- netMin=2 alone fixes #56 only (A unchanged, B 30/32); netMin=1 breaks A (78/2/5).
- Rolling-window or last-K impulse counts in place of the previous-session count: at least 3 errors, and #49 always wrong.
- A gap-size threshold in ATR only works between 8.2 and 15.1 ATR (#54 must stay normal), which is fit to one trade.
- A "far from VWAP" rule only works between 3.5 and 4.3 ATR and breaks A stops below that.

#49 has a long previous-session bias (+7), so no count-based bias can make it tight; only the opening gap below 10-09's whole range explains it. Note that its stop (8741) and time (11:55) are identical to #46 on 10-09, so a copy error cannot be fully ruled out, although 8741 also matches the tight formula's 8742.0.
